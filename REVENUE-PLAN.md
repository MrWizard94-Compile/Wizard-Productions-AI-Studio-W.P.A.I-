## 2. Why this order

- **RepoForge first** because its remaining work is *an account signup and an upload*. Every week it stays unlisted is a week of guaranteed $0.
- **MFM second** because it's the highest-margin product WPAI has and its buyers are findable in specific rooms (modding communities) rather than "the internet."
- **Stems third** because demand is real but discovery-dependent — they sell better once music/content traffic exists.
- **Music fourth in revenue terms only.** It launches on its own track (see MARKETING-PLAN.md) because it's the brand, the story, and the stem-pack funnel — but nobody should expect stream money in year one.

## 3. The one marketing motion that lifts everything

**The story: neurodivergent warehouse worker runs a one-man AI studio.** Local news (Buffalo/WNY), Amazon's own associate-story machine, AI/creator podcasts, build-in-public posts. Every placement carries ALL divisions at once (music + tools + book). 1 hr/week of pitching, ~1-in-20 hit rate, highest leverage-per-hour WPAI has.

## 4. Cross-division rules

1. **One storefront identity:** Gumroad under WPAI; every product page footer links the others + email list.
2. **Email list is company-wide** — a buyer of RepoForge hears about MFM and the EP. It's the only owned channel; capture from day one.
3. **Disclose AI involvement on everything** exactly as the music does — the transparency is the brand across all divisions, and quiet-AI products get review-bombed when discovered.
4. **Nothing new gets built until 1–3 are live.** New product ideas go to a parking-lot list. (Standing rule from the digital-products plan: distribution beats product count.)
5. Quality bar holds ("no generic AI slop") — one refund-magnet product poisons the storefront.
